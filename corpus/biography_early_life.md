# Early Life of Srinivasa Ramanujan

Srinivasa Ramanujan was born on 22 December 1887 in Erode, Tamil Nadu, India, at the residence of his maternal grandparents. His father, K. Srinivasa Iyengar, worked as a clerk in a sari shop, and his mother, Komalatammal, was a housewife who also sang at a local temple. The family lived in Kumbakonam, a town known for its ancient temples.

Ramanujan attended the Town High School in Kumbakonam, where he excelled in all subjects. However, his life changed dramatically in 1903 when he obtained a copy of G. S. Carr's *Synopsis of Elementary Results in Pure Mathematics*. The book contained around 5,000 theorems with minimal proofs. Ramanujan began to verify these results and soon moved on to his own discoveries, effectively teaching himself advanced mathematics in complete isolation.

Because he became so completely absorbed in mathematics, he neglected his other subjects. As a result, he failed his examinations at the Government Arts College in Kumbakonam and lost his scholarship. He later attended Pachaiyappa's College in Madras but again failed his non-mathematical subjects.

From 1906 to 1912, Ramanujan lived in extreme poverty, continuing his mathematical research independently. He recorded his findings in notebooks (now famous as Ramanujan's Notebooks) using a slate for his calculations to save paper, only recording the final results.

In 1909, he married S. Janaki Ammal. Seeking employment to support himself and his wife, he finally secured a clerical position at the Madras Port Trust in 1912 with a salary of £20 per annum. It was here that he was encouraged by his superiors, who recognized his genius, to write to mathematicians in England, leading to his historic 1913 letter to G. H. Hardy.
